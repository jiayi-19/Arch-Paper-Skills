# Author preferences and house rules

An author's explicit rules for their own paper override the general guidance in this skill. Ask for them
once at the start of substantial work, or read them from project instructions, and apply them to every
section.

Typical rules and how to honor them:
- **Punctuation.** Some authors forbid em dashes, en dashes or semicolons in paper text. Several prize papers
  in the full-paper notes use them heavily (the notes record counts per paper), so do not take sentence rhythm
  from those papers when the author forbids the marks. Split into separate sentences instead.
- **Register.** Formal written English, no colloquial phrasing. Plain direct sentences with a concrete subject.
- **Terminology.** Keep the author's names for components and never substitute a synonym.
- **Missing numbers.** Use a visible bracket placeholder. Never invent a value.
- **Process.** Some authors want the logic agreed in their own language before any English, one paragraph at
  a time, with drafts shown in chat and nothing pushed to a shared editor until they agree. Follow that.

## When the author wants undiluted claims

`claim-calibration.md` keeps a material cost or scope condition with a headline. An author may still ask for
claims without hedging words. Both can hold. Do not add "may", "somewhat" or "to some extent". State the claim
with its exact scope in its own words ("on 21 tasks", "against the best tuned baseline", "at RTL"), and report
costs and losses next to the number in the evaluation, as the prize papers in the full-paper notes do. A claim
whose scope is wider than its evidence is not made safe by deleting the scope. Record it in the claim ledger as
pending evidence and leave a bracket placeholder until the result exists.
