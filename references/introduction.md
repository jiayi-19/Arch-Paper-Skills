# Introduction and motivation

Build a paragraph chain whose order makes the contribution necessary:

1. What workload/machine question matters, under which objective?
2. What actually causes the bottleneck or misleading conclusion?
3. What does strong prior work already solve, and what cost remains?
4. What observation permits a different design or explanation?
5. What state, representation, action or interface implements that choice?
6. What evidence resolves the question, with costs and scope?

These are functions, not a fixed six-paragraph recipe. A contribution list can
summarize delivered objects/findings; it need not repeat every result sentence.
Retrieve source-located moves and text-free paragraph annotations before imitating a
surface style. The profile reports actual role sequences and their variability.

Separate Background/Motivation when readers need substantial conceptual grounding.
Background defines necessary objects; Motivation derives requirements; Method fulfills
them; Evaluation tests them. Credit closest work where the distinction matters.
Do not spend three sections repeatedly describing the same gap or organization.
