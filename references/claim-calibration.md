# Claim calibration and evidence map

Represent a substantial paper's claims as claim -> mechanism subsection -> test ->
source/locator -> conditions -> strongest wording -> unresolved support. User-supplied
results, code-observed behavior, scientific hypotheses and future experiments remain
different categories. The intake evidence ledger preserves those categories.

| Basis | Suitable claim | Scope obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Formal result | State the property proved. | Assumptions and precise theorem/property. |
| Controlled implementation or experiment | Report the observed effect of the intervention. | Fixed conditions, comparator, population and overhead. |
| Simulation/model | Report modeled behavior under supported semantics. | Coverage, model validation and missing interactions. |
| Observation/correlation | State the observation and an interpretation if useful. | Avoid turning correlation into a demonstrated cause. |
| Design rationale | Explain why the mechanism is expected to work. | Distinguish rationale from a measured benefit. |
| Planned/unavailable | Explicit draft marker or research question. | No invented result or completed-experiment tense. |

Assign a headline role to the few results carrying the main claim, supporting role
to evidence needed in Introduction, and table-only role to detailed measurements.
There is no fixed numeric quota. Keep a meaningful cost, failure regime or scope
qualification with the headline when it changes what readers would conclude.

Supported confidence and precise scope work together. Do not remove a critical
condition to sound stronger. Literature originality, expansion beyond initial knobs,
evaluator support and performance benefit require separate evidence.

An author may ask for claims without hedging words. See [author preferences](author-preferences.md) for how to
keep the scope exact without adding hedges.
